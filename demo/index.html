Try it

Three screens, one record. Click around.

Each demo runs on the same sample student: Maya Chen, a fictional Grade 11 player in British Columbia. The figures are worked out the way the real product works them out, from NCAA's September 2026 BC outline. Nothing you enter is saved, and reloading starts over.

The record itself. Open a course, add one, enter a report-card mark and watch the core GPA move. The five tabs, Profile, and every sheet and alert.

  • Due dates with urgency flags
  • Hockey hours beside the homework

A parent's page

Their child's standing the way an advisor sees it: the boxes, the core GPA, what's due and what won't count. Add a practice or a due date and see it wait for the phone.

  • School and hockey on one calendar
  • A seat on every video session
  • Report-card marks and documents

What the demos don't do. They don't sync with each other: each page loads Maya's record fresh. The advisor's portal isn't here, and nothing on any screen certifies eligibility; only the NCAA Eligibility Center does.
